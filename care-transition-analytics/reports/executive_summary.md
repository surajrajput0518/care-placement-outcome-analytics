# Executive Policy Briefing: Unaccompanied Children Care Transition Efficiency & Placement Outcomes

**Target Audience:** Office of the Secretary (HHS), Office of the Secretary (DHS), Director of the Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR), Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), House & Senate Judiciary Committees  
**Publication Date:** September 2026  
**Analytical Scope:** Complete Official Reporting Dataset (720 Reporting Days: January 12, 2023 – December 21, 2025)  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Core Paradox

Conventional oversight of the Unaccompanied Children (UAC) program assesses agency performance based on aggregate shelter occupancy. However, an operational analysis tracking daily transitions through the pipeline reveals an alarming operational paradox:

> **The Low-Inflow Operational Paradox:**  
> Despite border apprehensions dropping by **90.2%** in 2025 compared to 2023–2024, **transition efficiency degraded dramatically**. Average CBP custody dwell time rose by **147.6%** to **3.32 days (79.7 hours)**, breaching the statutory 72-hour *Flores Settlement Agreement* limit. In 2025, **38.5% of reporting days were non-compliant**, peaking at **73.3% in December 2025**.

Furthermore, average length of stay (LOS) in HHS shelter care exploded from **34.2 days** (2023–2024) to **226.9 days (~7.5 months)** in 2025 (+564.1%), indicating that residual populations face extreme case stagnation.

## 3. Root Cause Diagnostic

### Finding 1: Manifest Batching in Border Patrol Sectors
When daily apprehensions exceeded 100 children/day (2023–2024), CBP operated daily charter flights and interagency ground transit. When intake fell to 10–15 children/day in 2025, sectors delayed transfers to accumulate full transport manifests, holding children past the 72-hour statutory deadline in station cells unsuited for minors.

### Finding 2: The "Residual Caseload" Trap in ORR Shelters
The massive increase in HHS length of stay (from 34 to 227 days) does not reflect new arrivals; it reflects children who entered during peak periods but lacked immediate parental sponsors in the U.S. As shelter intake dropped, ORR case management resources were not successfully redeployed to resolve complex Category 2 (extended relative) and Category 3 (unrelated sponsor) home studies.

### Finding 3: Operational Batching Around Weekends
Transitions exhibit pronounced weekly surges: sponsor discharges cluster heavily on Thursdays and Sundays, while Tuesdays experience lowest activity. This indicates administrative processing bottlenecks rather than child-centered workflows.

## 5. Decision-Maker Summary

Reduced border crossings have not solved the humanitarian challenge—they have exposed deep-seated structural friction in transfer logistics and case resolution. By shifting operational focus from aggregate capacity monitoring to dynamic transition velocity, the federal government can eliminate statutory Flores violations and reunite children safely with vetted sponsors within weeks rather than months.
